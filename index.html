Step-by-Step Emergency First Aid Protocols

Basic first aid and safety guidance while professional help is on the way.

First Aid Basics & Medicine Safety

First aid is temporary support, not a diagnosis or replacement for a doctor. In a serious emergency, call 112 (or 108 ambulance service where available).

Scene safety & first steps

  • • Check for traffic, fire, electricity, violence or other danger; do not put yourself at risk.
  • • Check responsiveness and normal breathing. Ask someone to call emergency services and bring an AED/first-aid kit.
  • • If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR if you can and follow the emergency operator's instructions.
  • • For severe bleeding, use firm continuous pressure with clean cloth or gauze; do not remove embedded objects.
  • • Do not move someone with possible head, neck or spine injury unless the area is immediately dangerous.

Useful basic first-aid kit

Disposable gloves, sterile gauze, assorted plasters, roller bandage, medical tape, clean scissors, saline wound wash, a digital thermometer, and packaged oral rehydration salts (ORS).

ORS: Mix one sachet with exactly the amount of safe water stated on its packet. Offer small sips only to someone awake and able to swallow. Do not give anything by mouth to an unconscious or drowsy person.

Medicines: important precautions

  • • There is no general emergency medicine for every injury. Do not delay an ambulance or hospital visit to find or give medicine.
  • • Use over-the-counter medicines only according to the package label or a clinician/pharmacist; check allergies, expiry date, and other medicines first.
  • • Never give medicines, food or drink to an unconscious person, or to someone who cannot swallow safely.
  • • Do not give someone else's prescription, leftover antibiotics, or aspirin to a child; do not induce vomiting after suspected poisoning.
  • • Suspected poisoning, snakebite, severe allergy, major injury, pregnancy, or a child/infant needs urgent professional advice—not self-medication.
  • • Store medicines in original labelled packs, away from children, heat and moisture. Ask a pharmacist about safe disposal.

This general information cannot account for age, allergies, pregnancy or medical conditions. Follow emergency-dispatch and qualified healthcare professional instructions.

प्राथमिक उपचार केवल अस्थायी सहायता है; यह डॉक्टर की जाँच या इलाज का विकल्प नहीं है। गंभीर आपात स्थिति में 112 पर कॉल करें (या जहाँ उपलब्ध हो वहाँ 108 एम्बुलेंस सेवा पर)।

घटनास्थल की सुरक्षा और शुरुआती कदम

  • • पहले ट्रैफ़िक, आग, बिजली, हिंसा या किसी अन्य खतरे को देखें। खुद को जोखिम में न डालें।
  • • देखें कि व्यक्ति होश में है और सामान्य रूप से साँस ले रहा है या नहीं। किसी से आपात सेवा बुलाने और AED/फर्स्ट-एड किट लाने को कहें।
  • • यदि व्यक्ति बेहोश है और सामान्य साँस नहीं ले रहा, तो अपनी जानकारी के अनुसार CPR शुरू करें और आपातकालीन ऑपरेटर के निर्देश मानें।
  • • तेज़ खून बहने पर साफ़ कपड़े या गॉज़ से लगातार मज़बूत दबाव दें। घाव में फँसी वस्तु न निकालें।
  • • सिर, गर्दन या रीढ़ की चोट का शक हो तो व्यक्ति को न हिलाएँ—सिवाय इसके कि वहीं रहना तुरंत खतरनाक हो।

बुनियादी फर्स्ट-एड किट

डिस्पोज़ेबल दस्ताने, स्टरल गॉज़, अलग-अलग आकार की पट्टियाँ, रोलर बैंडेज, मेडिकल टेप, साफ़ कैंची, घाव धोने के लिए सलाइन, डिजिटल थर्मामीटर और पैकेट वाला ORS रखें।

ORS: पैकेट पर लिखी ठीक उतनी ही मात्रा के सुरक्षित पानी में पूरा पैकेट मिलाएँ। केवल होश में और सुरक्षित रूप से निगल सकने वाले व्यक्ति को छोटे घूँट दें। बेहोश या बहुत उनींदे व्यक्ति को मुँह से कुछ न दें।

दवाइयाँ: ज़रूरी सावधानियाँ

  • • हर चोट के लिए एक जैसी आपातकालीन दवा नहीं होती। दवा खोजने या देने के लिए एम्बुलेंस/अस्पताल जाने में देर न करें।
  • • बिना पर्चे की दवा भी केवल पैकेट के निर्देश या डॉक्टर/फ़ार्मासिस्ट की सलाह से लें। एलर्जी, एक्सपायरी और चल रही दूसरी दवाएँ जाँचें।
  • • बेहोश व्यक्ति या सुरक्षित रूप से निगल न सकने वाले व्यक्ति को दवा, खाना या पानी न दें।
  • • किसी और की पर्चे वाली दवा, बची हुई एंटीबायोटिक या बच्चे को एस्पिरिन न दें। ज़हर निगलने पर उल्टी न कराएँ।
  • • ज़हर, साँप का काटना, गंभीर एलर्जी/चोट, गर्भावस्था या शिशु/बच्चे की स्थिति में तुरंत चिकित्सा सहायता लें—खुद से दवा न दें।
  • • दवाइयाँ उनके लेबल वाले पैक में, बच्चों से दूर और गर्मी/नमी से बचाकर रखें। सुरक्षित निपटान के लिए फ़ार्मासिस्ट से पूछें।

यह सामान्य जानकारी उम्र, एलर्जी, गर्भावस्था या बीमारी को ध्यान में रखकर व्यक्तिगत सलाह नहीं देती। आपात सेवा और योग्य स्वास्थ्यकर्मी के निर्देश मानें।

Highest Priority Action

Snake Bite First Aid Protocol

Common snakes in UP belt: Krait, Cobra, Russell's Viper. Immediate transport is critical.

Call Ambulance 108

DO THIS IMMEDIATELY

  • 1. Keep the Victim Completely Still: Panic accelerates venom flow through lymphatics. Lay person down quietly.
  • 2. Immobilize Bitten Limb: Splint or support the bitten arm/leg at or below heart level using a rigid board or sling.
  • 3. Remove Tight Items: Strip off rings, watch, anklets, or tight clothing near bite site before swelling starts.
  • 4. Rush to Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) Hospital: Direct route to RMLIMS Hospital or Chandan Hospital immediately.

CRITICAL DON'TS (NEVER DO)

  • 1. DO NOT Cut Wound or Suck Venom: Sucking or cutting causes tissue necrosis, dangerous infection, and does not remove venom.
  • 2. DO NOT Apply Tight Tourniquet: Tight ropes or cords cut off arterial blood supply leading to limb loss/amputation.
  • 3. DO NOT Apply Ice, Chemicals, or Herbal Powders: Ice exacerbates local tissue destruction.
  • 4. DO NOT Give Alcohol or Aspirin: Aspirin thins blood and increases internal bleeding.
Note for Medical Staff / Escort: Note down time of bite, physical appearance of snake if safely seen (DO NOT capture or kill snake), and monitor breathing.
Hemostasis Emergency

Heavy Blood Bleeding Protocol

Uncontrolled bleeding can lead to fatal shock in minutes.

Call Ambulance 108

ACTION STEPS

  • 1. Direct Continuous Pressure: Place sterile gauze or cleanest available cloth over wound and push down firmly with both hands.
  • 2. Maintain Constant Pressure: Do not remove cloth to check wound. If blood soaks through, add another cloth layer on top.
  • 3. Elevate Bleeding Limb: Raise limb above heart level if no bone fracture is suspected.
  • 4. Apply Pressure Bandage: Wrap bandage firmly over dressing without cutting off circulation down limb.

WARNING & DON'TS

  • DO NOT Remove Embedded Objects: If knife, glass, or metal is stuck, press around it to stop bleeding; never pull object out.
  • DO NOT Clean Major Wounds First: Focus on stopping blood loss before trying to wash large arterial wounds.
  • Avoid Unsupervised Tourniquets: Use commercial tourniquets only on severe limb arterial spurting when direct pressure fails.
Road Trauma Response

Road Accident & Spine Immobilization

Faizabad Road Expressway accident protocols.

KEY STEPS

  • 1. Scene Safety: Turn on hazard lights, divert traffic, protect victim from incoming vehicles.
  • 2. Immobilize Head and Neck: Hold victim's head straight inline with neck. Assume cervical spinal injury until proven otherwise.
  • 3. Airway Check: Check if patient is conscious and breathing normally without bending their neck back.
  • 4. Call Local Police & Medical Dispatch: Dial 112 / 108 with your exact landmark (e.g. near the GITM main gate).

CRITICAL PRECAUTIONS

  • DO NOT Move Victim Unnecessarily: Moving a spinal injury victim can cause permanent paralysis or death.
  • DO NOT Remove Motorcycle Helmet: Unless victim is not breathing and airway is completely blocked.
  • DO NOT Give Water to Unconscious Person: Causes choking and airway obstruction.
Cardiac Lifesaving

Cardiac Arrest Hands-Only CPR

Perform when victim is unresponsive and not breathing normally.

COMPRESSION PROCEDURE

  • 1. Position Hands: Interlock fingers, place heel of hand in center of victim's chest (lower half of breastbone).
  • 2. Push Hard and Fast: Compress chest 2 inches (5 cm) deep at rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute (to the rhythm of "Staying Alive").
  • 3. Allow Recoil: Let chest rise completely between each compression.
  • 4. Continue Uninterrupted: Rotate pushers every 2 minutes until emergency ambulance team takes over.

IMPORTANT DON'TS

  • DO NOT Delay Starting CPR: Brain damage begins in 4 minutes without blood flow.
  • DO NOT Perform on Soft Bed: Place victim on hard flat floor or ground so compressions are effective.
Thermal First Aid

Burns & Scalds Management

COOLING STEPS

  • 1. Cool Immediately: Run cool clean tap water over burn area for 10 to 20 minutes.
  • 2. Cover Loosely: Place clean cling film plastic wrap or sterile non-fluffy cloth loosely over burn area.
  • 3. Keep Warm: Keep non-injured parts of patient warm to prevent hypothermia.

NEVER APPLY

  • NO Ice or Ice Water: Ice causes severe tissue damage and freezing burns.
  • NO Toothpaste, Butter, or Oils: Traps heat in tissue and increases wound infection risk.
  • DO NOT Pop Blisters: Intact blisters protect against bacterial infection.
Electrical Hazard

Electric Shock Safety & Relief

SAFE DETACHMENT

  • 1. Cut Power Source First: Switch off main breaker or pull socket plug before touching victim.
  • 2. Use Non-Conductive Object: If power cannot be turned off, stand on dry wooden board or rubber mat and push victim away using dry wooden stick or plastic pole.
  • 3. Check Vitals: Once detached, check breathing and pulse. Prepare CPR if heart stopped.

HAZARDS

  • DO NOT Touch Victim Bare-Handed: While they are still in contact with electrical current, you will be electrocuted.
  • DO NOT Use Metal or Wet Objects: Conducts electricity directly to rescuer.
Minor Wound First Aid

Hand Cut: Clean, Cover & Check

Micropore is adhesive medical tape to secure a dressing; it is not a medicine and should not be placed into the cut.

Serious injury? Call 112

For a small, shallow cut

  1. 1. Stop bleeding: Press a clean gauze pad or cloth firmly on the cut continuously. Raise the hand if comfortable.
  2. 2. Rinse: Once bleeding is controlled, gently rinse with clean running water. Wash the surrounding skin with soap; do not scrub inside the cut.
  3. 3. Cover: Pat the surrounding skin dry, put a sterile non-stick pad or clean plaster over the cut, then use Micropore tape on intact skin to hold the dressing. Keep it clean and change it if wet or dirty.
  4. 4. Check tetanus: If the cut is dirty/deep or vaccine status is uncertain, ask a clinician whether a booster is needed.

Medicines & when to get help

  • Micropore tape is not an antiseptic, antibiotic, or pain medicine. Do not put tape, powder, glue, or ointment inside the wound.
  • Do not pour alcohol, hydrogen peroxide, or strong antiseptic into the cut. Do not start antibiotic tablets or creams without a clinician/pharmacist's advice.
  • Get urgent care for bleeding that will not stop with firm pressure, a deep/gaping cut, a foreign object, numbness, trouble moving a finger, or a bite wound.
  • Seek medical advice if redness, warmth, swelling, worsening pain, pus, or fever develops. For a serious emergency call 112.
